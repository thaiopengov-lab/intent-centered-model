# Problem Statement

Modern governance, procurement, and system design processes often preserve procedures and documents while gradually losing the original intent that motivated them.

Projects may begin with a clear purpose, but over time their direction changes due to constraints, fragmented decisions, institutional routines, or hidden influence.

The visible process remains intact, yet the system becomes detached from the purpose it was meant to serve.

This phenomenon can be described as **intent drift**.

---

## Core Failure

The key failure is the loss of traceability between intent and what a system eventually becomes.

Systems preserve artifacts such as:

- documents
- implementations
- outcomes

But they often lose:

- the original intent
- decision rationale
- constraint influence
- actor involvement

---

## Why Transparency Is Not Enough

Transparency allows observers to see what happened at a particular moment.

However, transparency alone does not preserve relationships across time:

- why a decision was made
- which constraint shaped it
- how it related to the original intent

Traceability is required to reconstruct those relationships.

---

## Project Motivation

The Intent-Centered Model proposes a structure connecting:

- problems
- intent
- constraints
- actors
- decisions
- requirements
- implementation
- outcomes

The goal is not to create perfect systems, but to reduce the likelihood that complex systems become detached from their original purpose without leaving reconstructable traces.
