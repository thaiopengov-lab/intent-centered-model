# Glossary

This glossary defines key terms used in the **Intent-Centered Model (ICM)** and the **Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)**.

The purpose of this glossary is to maintain consistent interpretation of core concepts across documentation, specifications, and implementations.

---

# Problem

A **Problem** is the condition, issue, or failure that motivates the creation of a system, policy, or intervention.

A problem answers the question:

> Why does this system need to exist?

Examples:

- slow administrative processes
- lack of transparency in procurement
- fragmented information systems
- poor service delivery

A problem precedes and motivates intent.

---

# Intent

An **Intent** is the purpose or objective that a system is meant to fulfill in response to a problem.

Intent answers the question:

> What is this system trying to achieve?

Examples:

- reduce approval time
- improve procurement transparency
- enable traceable decision processes
- improve interoperability between agencies

Intent in ICM is assumed to be **evolvable but traceable**.

---

# Constraint

A **Constraint** is any condition that limits, shapes, or influences decisions.

Constraints may include:

- legal requirements
- budgets
- deadlines
- existing infrastructure
- organizational capacity
- regulatory frameworks

Constraints explain **why certain decisions were made despite limitations**.

---

# Actor

An **Actor** is an individual, group, or institution that participates in shaping a system.

Actors may:

- propose decisions
- approve decisions
- implement systems
- influence constraints

Examples:

- project owner
- procurement committee
- technical team
- vendor
- regulator

Actors help preserve **institutional accountability and context**.

---

# Decision

A **Decision** is a recorded choice that influences the design, structure, or direction of a system.

Examples:

- selecting an implementation approach
- approving procurement criteria
- revising project scope
- choosing an architecture

Decisions serve as the **bridge between intent and requirements**.

---

# Requirement

A **Requirement** is a formalized expectation derived from decisions.

Requirements describe what a system must do or satisfy.

Examples:

- systems must maintain audit logs
- workflows must preserve approval history
- systems must integrate with existing infrastructure

Requirements translate decisions into **implementable expectations**.

---

# Implementation

An **Implementation** is the realized system, policy, process, or artifact that fulfills requirements.

Examples:

- deployed software
- implemented workflow
- procurement document
- operational service

Implementation represents **what was actually built or executed**.

---

# Outcome

An **Outcome** is the observable result produced by an implementation.

Examples:

- processing time reduced
- transparency improved
- operational efficiency increased

Outcomes determine whether the original intent has been fulfilled, partially fulfilled, or contradicted.

---

# Traceability

**Traceability** refers to the ability to reconstruct relationships between elements across time.

In the context of ICM, traceability means being able to identify:

- which problem motivated the system
- which intent guided decisions
- which constraints influenced decisions
- which actors participated
- how requirements emerged
- what outcomes resulted

Traceability preserves **institutional memory and accountability**.

---

# Intent Drift

**Intent Drift** refers to the gradual divergence between a system's current behavior and its original intent.

Intent drift may occur due to:

- accumulated constraints
- fragmented decision-making
- organizational change
- hidden influence or capture

Not all intent drift is malicious. Some may represent legitimate adaptation. However, without traceability, it becomes difficult to distinguish between **legitimate evolution and harmful distortion**.

---

# Intent Evolution

**Intent Evolution** refers to the legitimate revision of intent in response to changing conditions.

Intent evolution should be:

- explicit
- documented
- traceable

This allows observers to understand how the purpose of a system has changed over time.

---

# Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)

The **Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)** is the structural representation used in the Intent-Centered Model.

It models relationships between:

- problems
- intent
- constraints
- actors
- decisions
- requirements
- implementation
- outcomes

The ITG represents these relationships as a **graph rather than a linear chain**, enabling systems to capture complex interactions and evolving contexts.

---

# Intent-Centered Model (ICM)

The **Intent-Centered Model (ICM)** is the conceptual framework that defines how intent traceability can be preserved in complex systems.

It proposes a structured relationship between:

- problems
- intent
- constraints
- actors
- decisions
- requirements
- implementation
- outcomes

The model's structural mechanism is the **Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)**.
