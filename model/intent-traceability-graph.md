# Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)

The **Intent Traceability Graph (ITG)** is the structural core of the **Intent-Centered Model (ICM)**.

It represents a traceable network connecting the elements that shape decisions in complex systems, including problems, intent, constraints, actors, decisions, requirements, implementation, and outcomes.

Unlike linear process models, the ITG treats these elements as a **graph of relationships**. This allows systems to preserve and reconstruct the traceability of intent across time.

---

## Purpose

The ITG exists to answer a fundamental question:

How can a complex system preserve the traceability of intent over time?

Many systems keep procedures and documentation but lose the reasoning that created them. The ITG provides a structure to preserve those relationships.

---

## Core Principle

**Traceability is not a chain. It is a network.**

Complex systems evolve through multiple actors, constraints, and decisions. Therefore traceability must be modeled as a **graph**, not a single sequence.

---

## Core Nodes

### Problem
The issue or condition that motivates action.

### Intent
The purpose or objective the system aims to fulfill.

### Constraint
Conditions that limit or shape decisions.

### Actor
People or entities participating in decisions.

### Decision
Recorded choices that affect the direction of the system.

### Requirement
Formal expectations derived from decisions.

### Implementation
The realized system, policy, process, or artifact.

### Outcome
The observable result produced by implementation.

---

## Why Graph Structure Matters

Real systems are not linear. One intent may produce many decisions, and decisions may be shaped by many constraints and actors.

A graph structure allows these relationships to be preserved and reconstructed later.
